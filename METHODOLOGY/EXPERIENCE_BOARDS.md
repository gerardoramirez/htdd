# Experience Boards

Experience Boards are the bridge between human understanding and product design.

They do not describe screens. They describe the emotional responsibility of each meaningful moment.

A board exists so a later designer, engineer, or agent cannot quietly replace the moment with a familiar product pattern.

---

## What every board must answer

- What human experience are we protecting?
- What should someone feel?
- What should the product intentionally refuse to do?
- What designed object replaces the ordinary product move?
- How should AI behave?
- What must engineering preserve?
- How do we know the experience succeeded?

The fourth question is as important as the third.

A refusal that only says what to avoid leaves the last screen empty. An empty screen reads as unfinished work. The team then fills it with the ordinary product move the refusal was meant to prevent.

---

## A refusal is not an empty screen

Name the ordinary product move this moment must not become.

Then name the positive form that holds the moment.

Residue can be atmosphere. It cannot be chrome.

If the designed object is missing, the board is not ready to govern a build.

---

## Named Product Gravity belongs on the board

The most useful build guidance is often not the feature list. It is the named failure mode.

> This moment becomes onboarding.
> This moment becomes a profile page.
> This moment becomes an idle state.

Once the failure mode has a name, ordinary product moves become visibly wrong before implementation begins: journals, continue buttons, “how do you feel” prompts, explanatory copy, empty-state UI.

---

## Emotional acceptance is not optional

Functional acceptance asks whether the screen works.

Emotional acceptance asks whether the human purpose was kept.

Both belong on the board. Engineering tests the first list. The team reviews the second. A green test does not close the second list.

---

## Boards stay living

Boards live at the project root. A milestone may reference them, record that they were reviewed, or keep a frozen copy when history requires it.

A milestone must not silently replace the living boards.

Observed, not doctrine: later implementation governance can refuse warmth a board still hopes for. If rank is not explicit at implementation time, agents and engineers will choose the warmer document. Record that conflict. Do not silently prefer warmth.
