# Experience Rhythm

Rhythm is how one beat becomes the next.

The visitor should not feel like they are moving through steps. They should feel the experience changing temperature.

A transition is allowed when the current moment has done enough work to protect the next one. The product may prepare, soften, fade, reveal, pause, or recede. It must not push, prompt, count, celebrate, nudge, or convert.

---

## What rhythm protects

No transition should require a call to action.

No transition should use progress, completion, or success language.

No transition should make the product more central than it was in the previous state.

The person becoming more present is not the product becoming more central.

---

## Readiness is the hard part

HTDD is unusually strong at telling a team what not to build. It is still less complete at telling a prototype when an emotional moment is ready.

Refusal patterns can keep a transition from becoming a task. They cannot, by themselves, prove that the next gift has arrived.

Observed from practice, not doctrine:

> Refusal patterns can prevent the wrong product, but they do not by themselves prove the right emotional experience.

---

## Time can proxy readiness. It is not readiness.

A prototype may use a time-based or settling-based fallback so the visitor is not trapped in a blocked task state.

That clock is a stand-in. If the visitor cannot linger, the fallback will feel like autoplay. Autoplay is a funnel.

Observed from practice, not doctrine:

> Time can stand in for readiness in a prototype, but it is not readiness. If the fallback cannot be lingered in, it will feel like a funnel.

A Transition Spec should say this warning out loud.

---

## Edge transitions are part of the rhythm

The first entrance and the later return are not ordinary handoffs.

They sit at the edge of the arc:

```text
Pre-Arrival -> Arrival
Continuation -> Return Arrival
```

They need their own governed shape. If they are split casually across unrelated state tickets, they will be rebuilt twice, and return behavior will leak into work that was not meant to own it.

Observed from practice, not doctrine.
